"As for the graphics card itself, the first thing to note is MSI's hybrid cooling approach.
The AIO cools the GPU and memory, but there is still a 100mm Torx 5.0 active fan built into
the shroud to cool the VRM and the rest of the PCB."

"Just next to the power connector is the dual-BIOS switch, with a choice between Gaming or
Silent modes. The Gaming BIOS is the default and this offers a power limit of 480W, higher
clocks and a more aggressive fan curve compared to the Silent mode, but we test both modes
later in this review."

"Looking at the radiator and AIO unit, this is manufactured by Cooler Master. The radiator
measures 27mm thick, but as the fans are 27.5mm thick, you'll need about 56cmm clearance in
your case. The sleeved tubing measures 47cm in length..."

"As for the two fans, these connect via standard 4-pin PWM headers to the small exposed
wires. This is good news as the fans can be easily swapped out for third-party models with
no proprietary headers to contend with. Just note that as MSI's included Silent Gale fans
are slightly thicker at 27.5mm, using standard 25mm fans will require shorter screws to
avoid puncturing the radiator fins. MSI says standard Asetek AIO screws will be fine, but
these are not supplied in the box."

[Photo captions present in the page, images not reproduced here: "fan-cables1.jpg",

"As for the cooler, MSI is using a copper baseplate which contacts both the GPU and the
VRAM, with the heat drawn away by the AIO cooler. The VRMs, however, make contact with the
black secondary heatsink, which is cooled by the fan on the shroud itself."

"Noise levels is an area where the Suprim Liquid X does well. Using the Gaming BIOS, we saw
noise levels hit 37dBa, and this dropped 2dBa using the Silent BIOS as that has a more
relaxed fan curve, for both the radiator fans and the fan on the card itself, so this is
definitely a key benefit to the AIO approach."

Pros/Cons summary: "Fans run nice and quiet." / "4-pin PWM fans are used and can be swapped
for third-party models." listed as pros; "Not enough radiator screws included." and
"Included fan screws don't allow for certain mounting configurations." listed as cons.

WHAT THIS ESTABLISHES:
1. The card's own shroud fan is stated as 100 mm in diameter, and its job is stated
   explicitly: it cools the VRM and "the rest of the PCB" — NOT the GPU die or memory, which
   are liquid-cooled. (Compare: Tom's Hardware states 95 mm for the same fan — see
   tomshardware-suprim-liquidx-review-page6.txt — the two reviews disagree on the exact
   diameter; kept as stated, not resolved.)
2. The two radiator fans use standard, swappable 4-pin PWM connectors — this corroborates
   the MSI forum owner's report of "4 pin connectors on the radiator" (see
   msi-forum-radiator-fan-problem.txt) from an independent source.
3. The AIO/radiator is stated to be manufactured by Cooler Master. This conflicts with
   Guru3D's page 2, which does not name a manufacturer but calls the fans "Gentle Typhoon"
   (see guru3d-suprim-liquidx-review-page2.txt) rather than MSI's own "MEG Silent GALE P12"
   naming (see the existing evidence file
   data/evidence/rtx4090-suprim-layout-2026-09-23/wccftech-suprim-liquidx-teardown.txt).
   Kept as a three-way disagreement, not resolved here.
4. Both the radiator fans and the card's own fan are confirmed, independently of the
   schematic, to run their own separate fan curves ("for both the radiator fans and the fan
   on the card itself") — i.e. they are not simply slaved together, which is consistent with
   NVML reporting two genuinely independent channels.

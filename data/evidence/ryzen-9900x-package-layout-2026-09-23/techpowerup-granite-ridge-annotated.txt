## Article text (verbatim, navigation and comments stripped)

High-resolution die-shots of the AMD "Zen 5" 8-core CCD were released and
annotated by Nemez, Fitzchens Fitz, and HighYieldYT. These provide a detailed
view of how the silicon and its various components appear, particularly the new
"Zen 5" CPU core with its 512-bit FPU. The "Granite Ridge" package looks
similar to "Raphael," with up to two 8-core CPU complex dies (CCDs) depending
on the processor model, and a centrally located client I/O die (cIOD). This
cIOD is carried over from "Raphael," which minimizes product development costs
for AMD at least for the uncore portion of the processor. The "Zen 5" CCD is
built on the TSMC N4P (4 nm) foundry node.

The "Granite Ridge" package sees the up to two "Zen 5" CCDs snuck up closer to
each other than the "Zen 4" CCDs on "Raphael." In the picture above, you can
see the pad of the absent CCD behind the solder mask of the fiberglass
substrate, close to the present CCD. The CCD contains 8 full-sized "Zen 5" CPU
cores, each with 1 MB of L2 cache, and a centrally located 32 MB L3 cache
that's shared among all eight cores. The only other components are an SMU
(system management unit), and the Infinity Fabric over Package (IFoP) PHYs,
which connect the CCD to the cIOD.

Each "Zen 5" CPU core is physically larger than the "Zen 4" core (built on the
TSMC N5 process), due to its 512-bit floating point data-path. The core's
Vector Engine is pushed to the very edge of the core. On the CCD, these should
be the edges of the die. FPUs tend to be the hottest components on a CPU core,
so this makes sense. The innermost component (facing the shared L3 cache) is
the 1 MB L2 cache. AMD has doubled the bandwidth and associativity of this 1 MB
L2 cache compared to the one on the "Zen 4" core.

The central region of the "Zen 5" core has the 32 KB L1I cache, 48 KB L1D
cache, the Integer Execution engine, and the all important front-end of the
processor, with its Instruction Fetch & Decode, the Branch Prediction unit,
micro-op cache, and Scheduler.

The 32 MB on-die L3 cache has rows of TSVs (through-silicon vias) that act as
provision for stacked 3D V-cache. The 64 MB L3D (L3 cache die) connects with
the CCD's ringbus using these TSVs, making the 64 MB 3D V-cache contiguous with
the 32 MB on-die L3 cache.

Lastly, there's the client I/O die (cIOD). There's nothing new to report here,
the chip is carried over from "Raphael." It is built on the TSMC N6 (6 nm)
node. Nearly 1/3rd of the die-area is taken up by the iGPU and its allied
components, such as the media acceleration engine, and display engine. The iGPU
is based on the RDNA 2 graphics architecture, and has just one workgroup
processor (WGP), for two compute units (CU), or 128 stream processors. Other
key components on the cIOD are the 28-lane PCIe Gen 5 interface, the two IFoP
ports for the CCDs, a fairly large SoC I/O consisting of USB 3.x and legacy
connectivity, and the all important DDR5 memory controller with its dual-
channel (four sub-channel) memory interface.

Sources: Nemez (GNR overview), Nemez (annotations), Fitzchens Fits (die-shots),
High Yield (YouTube)

## Note on the processor used

A companion article (WCCFTech, see wccftech-zen5-ccd-die-shots.txt in this
folder) states the die shots are of a Ryzen 5 9600X, "which features a single
Zen 5 CCD and a single IOD." The 9900X (two CCDs) is not itself pictured; this
article's package photo shows one populated CCD and one empty CCD pad on the
same substrate the 9900X uses.

## OBSERVATION — my own description of the article's images, labelled

I (the research agent) viewed the full-resolution images embedded in this
article directly (not saved to this repository; copyright). This is my own
observation of what the images show, separate from the article's text above,
which does not itself describe the geometric layout in these terms.

Image 1 — package photo, https://tpucdn.com/img/v0uxzdttqvW1dM1M.jpg
(labelled "Granite Ridge" package overview, annotated by Nemez):
The package substrate is a roughly square blue (teal) fibreglass board. The
top-left corner of the substrate, as the image is oriented, is bevelled/cut at
a 45-degree angle — a corner-key of the kind used for pin-1 / orientation
marking. The cIOD (larger die, labelled with "PCIe 5.0 x16 PHY", "Display
Engine", "128bit DDR5-5600 PHY", etc.) sits in the upper-middle area of the
substrate, below the cut corner. A single CCD (labelled "Zen 5 Core" x8 and
"L3$ 32MB") sits below the cIOD, in the lower-middle area of the substrate,
offset toward the left of the pair-of-CCD region. To the right of the
populated CCD, at the same height, there is a visible unpopulated
rectangular pad/outline on the substrate — the empty seat for a second CCD —
with no silicon die placed on it. So, in this image: cIOD above, the two CCD
seats below it side by side, and the cut/keyed corner is at the top-left,
above and to the left of the cIOD. I did not find any text source (from this
article or others fetched for this research) that names this bevelled corner
as "the golden triangle" or otherwise identifies which socket pin it marks;
this is an observation of the image only, not a documented claim.

Image 2 — bare CCD, https://tpucdn.com/img/o8fTKzZg4LzwrEPN.jpg
(annotated by Nemez): The CCD is drawn as a rectangle, taller than it is wide
in this crop. A vertical central strip is labelled "L3$ 32MB" (with a
narrower "V-Cache TSV Rows" callout running through it). To the left of that
strip are four rectangles stacked vertically, each labelled "Zen 5 Core". To
the right of the L3$ strip are another four rectangles stacked vertically,
each labelled "Zen 5 Core". So the eight cores are arranged as two vertical
columns of four, one column on each side of the shared L3 cache, not as two
horizontal rows. Along the bottom edge of the CCD rectangle, below both
columns and the L3$, is a horizontal band labelled "SMU/Power Management and
I/O Interconnect", and below that, two "IFOP PHY" blocks side by side, with a
"Test/Debug" block in the bottom-left corner. This picture does not indicate
which of the eight core positions map to which core ID the kernel/OS assigns,
or which four are the two that are fused off on a 6-core-per-CCD part like
the 9900X.

Image 3 — single-core zoom, https://tpucdn.com/img/AFnVIoGFWSCE6YXO.jpg:
zoom of one core's internal floorplan (Vector Regfile / FADD+FMAC lanes at the
left edge, Load/Store and Integer Execution in the middle, L2 Cache halves and
L2$ Tags at the right edge, facing where the L3 would be). This matches the
article's text describing the Vector Engine at the die edge and the L2 cache
facing the shared L3.

Image 4 — cIOD internals, https://tpucdn.com/img/xnHJeLERLtpzpqPm.jpg: internal
floorplan of the cIOD (PCIe PHYs, Display Engine, Media Engine, Memory
Controller, two IFOP PHY blocks along the bottom edge). Not used for a
position claim in the diagram; included for completeness.

Image 5 — photo of a delidded/diced die stack at an angle,
https://tpucdn.com/img/60z1u75rZfemT1Rr.jpg: a photographic close-up of
silicon at an oblique angle; the framing does not show the whole package
outline, so it does not by itself establish a layout claim beyond what image 1
already shows.
